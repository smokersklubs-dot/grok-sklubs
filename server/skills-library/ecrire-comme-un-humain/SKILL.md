# Write like a human

Readers spot machine-made text in two lines and stop trusting it. Write like someone with a point of view who knows the subject.

## Core rules

1. **Lead with the concrete thing**: an example, a number, a photo, a customer story, the result. Explain after.
2. **Proof, not adjectives**: "delivered in 24 h in Lyon" beats "a fast, reliable service".
3. **Short sentences**, then a longer one when it earns its place. Vary the rhythm.
4. **One idea per paragraph**, each section adds something new.
5. **Never invent** a fact, a figure, a quote, a customer review or a credential. If a fact is missing, ask or leave a visible gap.
6. **The owner's voice**: if their brand has a voice profile (see "Brand interview"), follow it; otherwise plain, warm, direct.

## Tics to remove

**Inflation and filler**: "in today's fast-moving world", "a true game-changer", "cutting-edge", "pivotal", "testament to", "it is important to note that", "in order to" (use "to"), "due to the fact that" (use "because").
**Machine vocabulary**: delve, crucial, enhance, foster, showcase, landscape, tapestry, vibrant, seamless, unlock, elevate, robust.
**Twisted grammar**: "serves as" or "stands as" instead of "is"; "it's not just X, it's Y"; everything in groups of three; changing the word for the same thing every sentence.
**Chatbot habits**: "Great question!", "I hope this helps!", "Feel free to…", "Let me know if…", a question at the end only to get engagement.
**Layout tics**: bold on every other word; "**Title:** text" lists that should be sentences; emojis in titles; long dashes everywhere (prefer full stops and commas); Title Case Headings.
**Vague sources**: "experts say", "studies show". Name the source or delete.
**Empty endings**: "The future looks bright." End with a fact, a next step or an offer.

**In French**, also: « Dans un monde en constante évolution », « véritable » et « incontournable » à tout bout de champ, « n'hésitez pas à », « plongeons dans », « il est important de noter que », « en somme », « un atout majeur », « au cœur de », « que vous soyez X ou Y », the "vous" when the brand says "tu" (and the reverse), anglicisms when a French word exists.

## Add a voice

Removing tics is half the job; a voiceless text is just as obvious.
- Have an opinion and react to facts.
- Say "we" or "I" when the owner speaks.
- Be specific: the street, the product, the hour, the price.
- Admit a limit when there is one ("we do not deliver on Sundays").

## By kind of text

- **Email**: the point in the first line, one request, a clear next step, a short signature.
- **Social post**: the hook in the first line, written for that network (see "Content per network").
- **Article or guide**: open with what the reader gets; one job per section; end with actions, not a soft summary.
- **Web page**: what it is, for whom, the price or the next step, visible without scrolling.

## Final check

Read the text and ask: "What makes this obviously machine-written?" Fix what remains. Check that every fact comes from the owner or a cited source.
