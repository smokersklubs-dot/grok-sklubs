# Parcels and returns

Laws and carrier conditions change and differ by country: before stating a deadline, check the carrier's conditions and the official source (in France, service-public.fr and the consumer code; in the EU, the right of withdrawal rules) and cite it. This is help to act, not legal advice.

## 1. Sort the delivery incident

- **Delay**: past the promised date. Check the tracking, ask the carrier, tell the customer before they ask.
- **Visible damage**: noted at delivery. Photos at once; the customer writes precise reservations on the delivery receipt when possible.
- **Hidden damage**: found after opening. Photos of the box, the packing and the item; the protest to the carrier has a short deadline (in France, a reasoned protest within a few days of delivery for parcels sent under French domestic transport law: check the carrier's conditions).
- **Missing items**: count, photos, weight on the label versus real weight.
- **Lost**: no tracking movement; open an investigation with the carrier after its stated delay.
- **Wrong address / refused**: find who is at fault (address entered by the customer or carrier error).

## 2. Claim or absorb

Gather: order, invoice (value), tracking, photos, packing description, customer's statement. Then decide with the owner:
- small amount and a good carrier: often cheaper to refund or resend and just note it;
- larger amount: file the claim within the deadline, follow up, accept a fair partial settlement;
- **the same carrier several times in a month**: treat it as a carrier problem whatever the amounts (talk to the account manager, compare carriers).
The customer is served first (resend or refund as the shop's policy says); the claim with the carrier runs in parallel.

## 3. Returns

- **Policy**: online sales in the EU give consumers a withdrawal period (14 days in France, with exceptions such as made-to-measure or perishable goods) and the shop refunds within the legal deadline; the legal guarantee of conformity applies on top. Write the shop's policy in plain words and apply it the same way to everyone.
- **Grade the returned item**: A like new (resell as new), B small marks or damaged packaging (resell as "open box" or with a discount), C visible wear (outlet, second-hand, discount), D not working or incomplete (parts, recycling). Photograph on arrival.
- **Abuse** (worn then returned, a different item in the box, very frequent returns): look at the facts calmly, note the evidence, and let the owner decide; never accuse a customer in writing on a suspicion. Many frequent returners are just unsure of sizes: a size guide or an exchange may help more than a refusal.
- **Supplier**: a defective batch → gather the cases and claim from the supplier.

## 4. Talking to customers

Short, warm, precise: what happened, what we do, by when, nothing they must chase. Never promise what the policy does not give without the owner's approval. A refund, a resend or a gesture commits money: prepare it, the owner approves (unless a rule they set covers that exact case).

## 5. Keep track

A simple log (file or spreadsheet): date, order, incident, carrier, cost, outcome, time to resolve. Monthly: incidents by carrier, return rate by product and reason, money recovered. Products with many returns for the same reason point to a description, size or quality problem to fix.
